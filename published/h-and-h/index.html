Where this starts

This report is a starting line, not a scorecard. It is worth reading it that way, because the numbers in it describe a business that has just changed into a different one.

You are close to invisible in search right now. None of the ten searches we are targeting return your website. Your Google listing appears in five of the forty-nine places we checked across the county, none of them further than about six miles from your front door, and its best position among them is 14th. Advertising produced no genuine enquiries in July. We are not going to soften any of that.

What matters is why, because the reason is not that the work is failing. July measures a business that no longer exists. H&H spent years as a handyman company, and Google's picture of you is still mostly that company: the two most-seen searches bringing up your site in July were palm beach fl handyman and small job carpenter near me. Meanwhile the new website pages, the ones actually built to win remodeling searches, went live on 31 July, so July contains essentially none of them. And your Google listing still described a handyman business until this week.

So there was no version of July that was going to look good, and a report claiming otherwise would be lying to you. The useful question is whether the right things are now in place to change it. Most of them went in during the last week of July and the first week of August, which is what the rest of this report covers: what got built, what we are aiming it at, and what should start moving first.

The single biggest change happened this week

Your Google listing was still categorised as a handyman, electrician, painter, plumber and screen repair service. Those categories are the strongest signal Google uses to decide which searches to show you in, so it was showing you for handyman work and not for remodeling. That is now fixed, and the main category is Bathroom remodeler. It is the correct choice: bathrooms are the only service converting in advertising and the largest remodeling search in your market. None of July's numbers benefit from it, and August's should begin to.

      A column of "not ranking yet" is the correct reading of a site whose pages went live on the last day of the month. New pages do not arrive in the top ten. They arrive somewhere past position 30, or nowhere at all, and then climb as Google gathers evidence that the site deserves to be there. That evidence is links, mentions, consistent business information and time.

      The sequence we expect, and the one you should hold us to, is: appear at all, then climb, then earn clicks, then produce enquiries. Each step takes weeks and they happen in that order. Reading the click count in August and concluding it has not worked would be reading the wrong number at the wrong time. The number that should move first is how many of these ten searches your site appears for at all, at any position.

      Location pages, city by city

      Local searches are won one city at a time. Someone searching bathroom remodeling west palm beach sees a different set of results from someone searching the same words in Boca Raton, and Google strongly prefers a page written for that specific city over a general one that mentions it in passing. So a large part of the build has been a dedicated page for each service in each city worth winning, rather than one page trying to cover the county.

      There are nine of these live now, alongside the main service pages. All eight city pages also had real customer reviews added to them on 3 August, which helps visitors and gives Google more reason to associate you with each town.

The two that have started moving are the ones that have existed longest. Watching this table fill in is the clearest month-by-month measure of whether the location work is paying off.

      Why we are not building more pages

      Every service and city we want to win now has a page, verified against the live site. The gap is not content any more, so adding pages would be work that cannot pay off. What is missing is authority: your site has a fraction of the pages, links and directory presence of the established remodelers you are competing with. That is what August's work is aimed at, and it is a slower, less visible kind of job than building pages.

At the same time the number of different searches your site appears for went from 52 in May to 94 in June to 120 in July, and the number of your pages earning any visibility went from 11 to 26.

Adding a lot of low positions drags the average down, from 12.6 to 16.9, and almost nobody clicks a result that far down. Growing surface area with a falling average position is exactly what the first stage of this process looks like.

      It is also the stage that can be mistaken for failure, so here is the line we will hold ourselves to: query count rising while clicks fall is fine through August. If it is still true in late September, something is wrong and we will say so.

      Why your clicks cost what they do, and the fix we are testing

      Google scores every keyword out of 10 on how well the advert and the page it leads to match what was searched. Every bathroom keyword with enough data is scoring 1 to 3. That score multiplies what you pay, so you are paying more than a competitor for the same position on the page.

      The likely cause is straightforward: the bathroom advert has been pointing at an older standalone landing page rather than the bathroom page rebuilt on 31 July. Repointing it is the first thing we are testing in August, and it is the cheapest lever in the account.

      A correction to the enquiry count you may have seen

      The account shows 8 paid enquiries across 2026. We checked every one on 5 August and 5 were our own tracking tests, submitted by us to confirm your forms and call tracking worked. The genuine total is 3, and July's is nil. We would rather give you the smaller true number than a larger one we cannot stand behind. The test entries are being flagged so they stop appearing.

      This is the baseline, measured before the category fix could have any effect. For bathroom remodeling your listing comes back reliably from five of the 49 points, all of them within about six miles of the office. Two of those five sit over the Atlantic, where nobody is actually searching from, so the meaningful footprint is three points on land: 2 miles south-east at 29th, 3.3 miles north-east at 14th, and 3.7 miles north-west at 40th. Kitchen remodeling holds up at exactly one point, in 32nd. Tub to shower conversion did not repeat anywhere.

      So the whole of your Maps presence is a handful of points within a few miles of the front door, none of them on the first page. Everywhere else across the county, Wellington and Royal Palm Beach included, the listing does not come back at all.

      Until this week your listing told Google you were a handyman, so it is not surprising that it was not being offered for remodeling searches. That is precisely the signal that has just changed, and this map is the measurement we will repeat at the end of August to find out how much it was worth. Running the identical scan again is the cleanest before-and-after test available to us.

      One number we want to understand better

      Fifty direction requests against a single phone call is an unusual shape, particularly for a remodeler, where customers do not drive to your address. It may be people checking you are genuinely local before enquiring, which would be a good sign. It may be an artefact of how the address is recorded. We are not drawing a conclusion from one month.
