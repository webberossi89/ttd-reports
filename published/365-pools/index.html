What we have changed

Following up on our conversation, here is what has already been actioned on the account and what is queued behind it.

Done 5 August: the budget has moved back to the Search campaign

Search goes from $30 to $52 a day. Performance Max goes from $20 to $13 a day. Four fifths of the budget now sits behind Search, reversing the reallocation we ran as a test from June.

The daily figures rise, but your monthly spend does not. Because the ads now run weekdays only, they serve about 21.75 days a month instead of 30, so the daily budget has to go up simply to spend the same amount over fewer days. We sized it against what this account actually spends per serving day rather than the theoretical maximum, which models to about $1,505 a month against your $1,500 target.

The test was worth running and we would run it again. In June we moved budget from Search into Performance Max to see whether the cheaper channel would buy more actual jobs. For one month it looked promising. Held for a second month, it did not hold up: July produced more contacts and fewer qualified jobs, and the qualified enquiries that did arrive leaned toward the campaign we had taken money away from.

Two numbers decided it. Enquiries that came through the website, which is overwhelmingly Search traffic, qualified at 38%. Enquiries from the Performance Max call button qualified at 20%. And Search was losing a third of its available impressions purely because it ran out of budget each day, while Performance Max was never budget-limited. We were rationing the better traffic to fund the weaker traffic.

Also done 5 August: filtering out the searches that were never going to become jobs

Performance Max had no keyword filters on it at all, which is why it was picking up job applications, DIY questions and people shopping for pool chemicals. We added 54 filters across both campaigns covering job seekers, do-it-yourself searches, pool supply and chemical shopping, above-ground pools, and new-build or resurfacing work you refer out. This does not reduce your reach for repair work. It stops you paying for clicks that were never going to book.

The rest of this report is the evidence behind those decisions, what we found in the account that still needs fixing, and the two things we cannot fix from inside the ad account.

What we found that needs a bigger fix

Your Search ads currently send everyone to the homepage, whatever they searched for. Someone searching “pool pump replacement” and someone searching “pool heater repair” land on the same general page. The service pages on your site do exist, and when a click does reach one it converts far better: $35.61 and $45.05 per enquiry, against $67.96 for the homepage. They currently receive about a tenth of the spend.

Rebuilding the campaign so each service has its own ad pointing at its own page is the largest improvement available inside the account. It is a bigger job than a settings change, so it is scheduled rather than done, and it is the main piece of work for the coming month.

The month behind the decision

July cost almost exactly what June cost, and it produced more contacts but fewer real jobs. Spend was $1,454.48 against $1,462.81 in June. Tracked leads went up, from 52 to 59. But when your team reviewed those leads in the lead sheet, the number of paid enquiries you marked as qualified fell from 23 to 15, and booked appointments went from 10 to 9.

That moves the number that actually matters. In June a qualified paid enquiry cost $63.60. In July it cost $96.97.

Not all of that is the budget split, and the budget change on its own will not fix all of it. Of the 39 paid enquiries your team marked as not qualified, 15 were hang-ups or calls with nobody on the line, and another 7 came in outside office hours. That is 22 of the 54 paid contacts for the month, roughly two in five, that never became a conversation. Those are the ones we cannot reach from the ad account, and they are covered at the end.

The ad call button is the weak link

Enquiries that came through the website qualified at 38%. Enquiries from the call button on the ad qualified at 20%, and that block is where nearly every hang-up sits. A tap-to-call button is the easiest thing in the world to press by accident and the easiest to abandon, and it puts the caller straight through with no context. It still produced 6 qualified enquiries and 4 appointments, so it is not something to switch off, but it should not be judged on raw lead count.

A note on the closed jobs and the fees

Your sheet records 3 jobs closed in July and $879.00 collected across 11 entries, of which $435.00 sits on paid enquiries. Almost all of that is $69 and $99 diagnostic fees rather than repair revenue. The Notes column is the only place money gets recorded, so anything invoiced later and not written back into the sheet is invisible to this report. If you want the report to carry real job value, writing the repair total into Notes when the job closes is all it takes.

What the 39 unqualified enquiries actually were

Grouped from your team’s own notes on every paid enquiry marked not qualified in July.

Hang-ups, at 15, are the single biggest loss in the account. They are also the one category no bid, budget or keyword change touches. These are people who wanted a pool repair company enough to click an ad and dial, and then rang off. Every one of them is a job that was available and did not happen.

Seven calls came in outside office hours. Both campaigns are scheduled to stop advertising in the evening, so these are people finding you and calling at a time nobody is there to answer. An after-hours voicemail that promises a callback by a stated time, or a simple overflow number, would convert a portion of these.

Seven were outside the service area. The campaigns target Brevard County, and only people physically in Brevard, so this is not a targeting fault in the usual sense. Brevard runs roughly 70 miles from Titusville to Micco, and parts of it are outside the zone you will actually send a truck to.

The remaining categories, wrong numbers, existing-customer admin and one job seeker, are normal background noise for a service business and not worth chasing at this volume.

Two findings, and the budget move on 5 August addresses the first of them.

The campaign was capped by budget, not by competitiveness. It lost a third of its available impressions in July purely because the daily budget ran out, against only 7.3% lost to being outranked. In plain terms, there is roughly another 50% of qualified pool repair demand in Brevard that we are eligible to appear for and were switching off partway through each day. Taking the daily budget from $30 to $40 is aimed squarely at that third. It will raise Search spend and it will raise the cost of the account’s cheapest-looking leads, because Search costs more per lead than Performance Max. It should also raise the number of enquiries worth quoting, which is the trade we are making deliberately.

The second finding was about when the ads run, and it is now fixed. You told us not to advertise on Sundays because the phones may not be answered. That was the right instruction, and applying the same test to the rest of the week showed the schedule was much wider than your working day everywhere else too.

Before today the Search campaign advertised 7am to 8pm Monday to Friday plus all day Saturday, and Performance Max advertised 8am to 8pm seven days a week. Both were paying to be visible late into the evening and across the weekend, when nobody was there to pick up. Over the last three months Search alone spent $645.82 in those hours: $384.11 in the early-morning and evening bands, and $261.71 on Saturdays. That is also where the seven after-hours calls in July came from. They were not bad luck. The schedule was doing exactly what it had been set to do.

Both campaigns now run Monday to Friday, 9am to 7pm: starting when you open, and staying on for two and a half hours after you close.

The evening tail is deliberate. You are right that somebody can still fill in a form after hours, and the data says the evening is exactly where that happens. Over the last three months the 5pm to 8pm band cost $71.26 per enquiry, better than the campaign average of $79.55. The 7am to 9am band cost $174.39, for the same number of enquiries. Early-morning clicks were people ringing a phone before anyone was in; evening clicks were people at home dealing with a problem. So the margin now sits on the evening side only.

This also explains the most expensive hour we found in the whole account. 8am was costing $237.84 per enquiry, roughly three times the campaign average, and we could not account for it until you confirmed you open at 9. It was an hour of clicks arriving at a closed office. It is now switched off.

The search terms themselves are clean

We reviewed every term the Search campaign paid for in July. The top spenders were “pool repair near me”, “pool pump repair near me”, “pool service near me” and “pool pump replacement”. There is no junk to negative out and no budget leaking to irrelevant searches. This campaign’s problem is that it is too small, not that it is poorly aimed.

Why Performance Max was cut back

Its lead count is real. A large and growing slice of it is people who already knew your name.

Google reports which search themes drove a Performance Max campaign. In July, 23.5 of Performance Max’s 75 reported conversions came from people searching “365 pools” or “365 pool service inc”. Those are people who already know your name and were looking for you specifically. In June it was 27%, so the share is growing.

This matters because it is the main reason Performance Max looks so cheap. Somebody typing your business name was always going to find you. Counting those as new leads flatters the campaign and makes the $12.15 cost per lead look like a bargain next to Search’s $94.08. Once you strip out the branded conversions, the two campaigns are far closer than the headline suggests, and the case for funding Performance Max ahead of Search largely disappears. That, plus the 20% qualified rate on its call button, is why its daily budget came down to $10 rather than being left where it was.

It stays on at $13 a day rather than being switched off, because it still produced 6 qualified enquiries and 4 appointments in July, and because it covers Maps and YouTube, where Search cannot reach.

What else Performance Max is showing for

Beyond your own name, the largest themes were “pool service titusville fl”, “brevard pools inc”, “pinch a penny near me”, “intercoastal pools” and “leslie pools melbourne fl”. Appearing when somebody searches a competitor by name is normal and worth keeping. It is the one place in this account where the automation is genuinely earning its keep.
