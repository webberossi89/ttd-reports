The month in short

July was the first month the Google Ads campaign was live. It ran for 26 days, spent $463.53, and brought in 20 tracked leads at $23.18 each. Across every channel the phone and the forms produced 59 enquiries, of which 43 were genuine, quotable jobs.

Two things shaped the month. On 14 July the service area was narrowed from seven counties to Westchester and Putnam at your request, and paid volume fell sharply for about a week. On 21 July we added tuning coverage, which was the single most searched service in the account and had no keyword pointing at it, and volume recovered.

The most useful finding came out of reviewing where the clicks were landing. The two pages the advertising most needed, one for selling a piano and one for tuning, did not exist on the site. People searching to sell a piano were being sent to the appraisals page, which has no form on it at all. Both pages have now been built and the ads point at them. That work happened on 5 August, so none of its effect is in these numbers.

Rankings improved, clicks did not. Here is why

Ford Piano is being shown in Google far more often than in May, and at a better average position. Clicks went the other way, down from 154 to 132. That looks contradictory until you look at what the new impressions are for.

The growth is coming from research questions and out of area searches, not from people looking to buy. In July the site was shown 271 times for damper replacement piano, 143 times for full piano rebuild process, and more than 400 times across a cluster of Mason & Hamlin searches from New Hampshire. Every one of those returned zero clicks, and they sit at positions 16 to 22 where almost nobody clicks anyway.

So the visibility is real but it is landing on searches that cannot become customers. This is exactly why the plan concentrates on local Westchester sales and rebuilding terms rather than chasing national piano-encyclopedia traffic, and it is the clearest evidence yet that the focus is the right one.

One fix worth making: your home page is five different addresses

The home page earned 59 clicks in July, 45% of everything the site received. Those clicks are split across seven different versions of the same address: with and without www, over http and https, and two variants carrying tracking tags from the Google Business Profile link.

Google is treating these as separate pages, which splits the page's earned authority instead of pooling it. Consolidating them onto one address is a contained technical job and it strengthens the single most valuable page on the site. We have added it to the queue.

Where you show up on the map

Rankings change depending on where the person searching is standing. To see that, we scanned a grid of 49 separate locations spread across an 18 mile square centred on the showroom, and recorded Ford Piano's position in the Google Maps results at every one of them. Each circle below is one of those locations. Green is a strong position, red a weak one.

Each square covers about 18 miles across, reaching Peekskill on the western edge and Katonah on the eastern one. The ringed circle in the middle is the showroom, and the number in each circle is the position, so the colour is a shortcut rather than the only way to read it. Scanned 5 August 2026.

What the map says

This is a strong local position. Ford Piano appears in the Maps results at 146 of the 147 points scanned across the three searches, and holds a top three place at 121 of them. At the showroom itself it ranks first on all three. Piano repair near me is the strongest, in the top three at 45 of 49 points and averaging 1.8.

The map is green through the middle and softens at the outer corners, most of all to the south west, toward the river and the approach to Ossining, where sell my piano falls to seventh and eighth. A wider scan we ran alongside this one confirms it keeps weakening further out in that direction.

That matches what the keyword tracking shows, a business that owns its home town and thins out across wider Westchester County, and it arrives from a completely independent measurement. Two methods pointing at the same place is a good reason to trust it, and it says the county-wide content work is aimed correctly.

A spam cluster, and why it is not in your numbers

Six of the calls tagged to paid search arrived on 30 July from an automated caller claiming to be from the Google Ads team. They carry no click identifier and Google Ads correctly recorded no conversions that day, so they never entered the lead count. Setting them aside, 20 of 22 genuine paid enquiries were quotable, which is 91%.

Two revenue lines earning without ads

Piano moving and piano rentals are both kept out of the paid campaign deliberately. Both still produced quotable work in July without it: two moving jobs quoted at $550 and $500 to $600, and a rental at around $2,500. Moving in particular is the strongest non-brand search the site already earns, so we will pursue it through content and rankings rather than advertising.
