The short version

Google Ads will tell you conversions went up 71% in July. That number is misleading, and we would rather explain it than take credit for it.

In early July we connected your Google Business Profile phone number to call tracking. From that point on, calls placed from your Google Maps listing started being recorded. They were always happening — we simply could not see them before. Those newly visible calls account for 69 of the 99 extra conversions. They are not new business; they are business that was previously invisible.

Strip that change out and compare like with like: conversions went from 120.5 to 150.5, a genuine 25% improvement on 2.7% less spend. That is a good month. It is just a smaller and more honest number than the one the platform reports.

The measure we care about most is new-customer conversations, which we can now count because your lead sheet is being tagged. Paid search produced 13 new-customer conversations in July, against 11 in June, at $247 each rather than $300. Modest, real, moving the right way.

Where the conversions actually came from

Four different things get counted as a “conversion” in your account. Separating them is the only way to see what changed.

What this means for comparisons

August will be the first month where both halves of the comparison are measured the same way. Until then, judge the account on calls from ads (90 → 122) and on new-customer conversations (11 → 13), not on the headline conversion total.

What went right

Propane cost per click fell 31%, from $12.91 to $8.91, and it delivered more conversions on 27% less money. The competitor and “grill refill” negative keywords we added are doing their job: the campaign is no longer paying for people who want a 20 lb tank filled.

Air conditioning conversions nearly doubled, 11 to 20, in the middle of its season. Cost per click rose to $18.35, which is the price of competing for AC repair in Westchester in July, but the conversion rate rose faster than the price did.

What needs attention

You are now losing 63% of available impressions to budget, up from 44% in June. In plain terms, your ads were eligible to appear roughly five times as often as they did, and the daily caps stopped them. That is not automatically a reason to spend more — it depends on the answer to the budget question at the end of this report.

Roughly half of paid leads are existing customers. They are worth serving, but they would have called you anyway. That is the single biggest reason the raw conversion count overstates how much new business the advertising is creating.

Who is actually calling

Your lead sheet now tags almost every call. This is the most useful thing in the report, because it converts a conversion count into a customer count.

Reading the three groups

New customer — someone who has not used you before, asking about work. 13 in July, 11 in June. This is the number the advertising exists to produce.

Existing customer — service calls, scheduling, an oil or propane top-up. Real revenue, but not acquisition.

Other — billing questions, messages for a specific person, job applicants, sales calls, wrong numbers, and out-of-scope requests such as barbecue-grill propane refills.

A caveat we want to be straight about

Only 29 of July’s 136 paid leads carry a click ID, which is the only reliable way to trace a lead back to a specific campaign. The rest arrive as phone calls with no click behind them. So these figures are trustworthy at the channel level and not at the campaign level, and we have deliberately not published a per-campaign new-customer breakdown that the data cannot support.

The same caution applies to the April and May tagging, which was too sparse to compare. June is the first month with usable coverage, which is why this report compares July to June only.

Organic search

Clicks are flat and rankings are steadily improving. Those two facts are not in conflict, and the reason they can both be true is the most important thing on this page.

The homepage did not lose a third of its traffic

The homepage looks like it fell from 223 clicks to 141. It did not. In July the link on your Google Business Profile was tagged so we could measure it, which split the homepage into two entries in Google’s reporting. Add them back together and it is 224 clicks in July against 223 in June. Flat, not down.

On 8 July we added emergency-plumbing and AC-repair question sections to your plumbing and cooling pages, aimed squarely at this list. The rankings responded: 24 hour plumbers near me moved from position 16.4 to 11.5, ac repair dobbs ferry from 4.1 to 2.0, ac repair from 41 to 7.8.

And not one of them produced a single click. That is not a failure of the content. On local searches like these, the top of the page is taken up by ads, the map pack and an AI summary before a single ordinary result appears. Ranking second in the blue links can still mean being below the fold.

This is why the work has shifted toward your Google Business Profile. For these searches, the map pack is the page, and getting into it is worth more than another two positions in the standard results.

Where the listing was seen

Views up 18%, actions down 7%

The listing was shown 731 times against 620 in June, and the growth is almost entirely mobile search, which rose from 103 to 174. The four categories we added in July — air conditioning contractor, heating oil supplier, propane supplier and generator shop — are the most likely reason you are surfacing for a wider range of searches.

But actions fell from 463 to 431, so the share of viewers who called, asked for directions or clicked through dropped from 75% to 59%. A wider net catches more people who are only browsing, so some of this is expected. It still needs watching, because the listing is now the main event for the searches in the previous section.

Reviews are the lever we are not pulling

You are at 4.3 stars from 86 reviews. Sixty-nine of them are five stars. The rating is held down by eleven one-star reviews, which is nearly one in eight.

Competitors sitting at 4.7 or above will win the click even when you rank alongside them. You gained exactly one review in July. A steady trickle of genuine five-star reviews is the cheapest available improvement to every number in this report, and it needs a process rather than a one-off push.
