What the 13 disqualified leads tell us

Eleven of the thirteen were people looking for household bin collection, not a dumpster. Callers wanting a missed garbage pickup chased up, asking about weekly residential service, or trying to pay a bill they thought belonged to another local hauler. It is a persistent pattern in this market: people searching for waste services do not always distinguish a roll-off rental company from a sanitation route.

That is a solvable advertising problem rather than a sales one, and we acted on it in early August. Eleven new negative keyword rules now block that traffic before it costs anything, covering the general "waste management" family and the local hauler names those callers were actually looking for.

The seventeen unanswered voicemails all rang outside opening hours, which is why they cannot be counted either way.

The honest position is that organic search is early and the click numbers are small. The site appeared in Google results 1,265 times in July and earned 29 clicks. Average ranking position was 51.6, which is roughly page five, and page five does not generate clicks no matter how good the listing is.

What matters more at this stage is the direction. The first six days of August already produced 305 impressions, an annualised pace well ahead of July, and average position has edged up to 48.4. Google is indexing and testing the site across a widening set of searches, which is exactly what should happen in months two and three.

Your money page, /dumpster-rental/, is the clearest example: 531 impressions in July but only 5 clicks, because it is sitting at position 49. The page is being seen by Google and shown to people. It is not yet ranked high enough for them to click it. The work in August is aimed squarely at moving that position.

What we did

July and early August

Advertising

  • Reviewed every lead individually and confirmed which were real opportunities.
  • Added 11 negative keyword rules to stop the household bin collection traffic described above.
  • Moved the "Dumpster Sizes & Pricing" ad link to the main dumpster rental page. It had been sending your most interested visitors to a page that quoted a price without giving them a way to act on it.
  • Retired the "Areas We Serve" ad link, which was the most expensive destination in the account per enquiry.
  • Confirmed all eight ads are approved and running, including the two construction ads that were in review.
  • Tidied the conversion tracking so reported lead numbers reflect one lead per lead.

Website and organic

  • Published four new guides covering permits, what can go in a dumpster, mattress disposal and estate cleanouts.
  • Rewrote the dumpster rental page title to lead with pricing, to earn more clicks at whatever position it holds.
  • Fixed a duplicated business name across 28 page titles that was wasting space in Google results.
  • Corrected page titles and descriptions that were being cut short in search results.
  • Brought the blog index up to date so all seven published guides are reachable.
  • Removed low-value author pages from Google's index so they stop competing with real pages.

August priorities

1. Reach $10,000 in spend before 21 August. This unlocks the $20,000 credit and is the highest-value action available to the account this month. On current pace we expect to cross it around 19 August. We are checking daily.
2. Get the Google Business Profile verified. Local searches put a map of nearby businesses above the ordinary results, and an unverified profile cannot appear there or collect reviews. Until it is verified, that entire route to enquiries is closed to you, so this is a priority for the month.
3. Hold cost per quotable lead near $75. The negative keywords added in early August should help by removing spend on calls that were never going to become jobs.
4. Move the dumpster rental page off page five. Internal linking and continued publishing are aimed at this. It is the page most likely to turn organic visibility into actual enquiries.
5. Review bidding after 17 August. Conversion tracking changed at the start of August, and we want a clean run of data before making bidding changes rather than reacting to a partial picture.
