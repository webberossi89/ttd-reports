The month in short

In July the advertising spent $1,084.56 and produced 10 tracked enquiries. Every one has since been listened to or read and judged individually, and six were real, workable jobs, at $180.76 each. That is the figure we will hold the advertising to from here, because a raw enquiry count can be flattered by robocalls and wrong numbers and a real job cannot.

The month had two halves. The GPR and concrete cutting campaign went live on 2 July with its own two landing pages, and in its first four weeks it produced half of the month's enquiries. The demolition campaign produced the other half.

Organic search was flat. The site earned 35 clicks from Google, against 33 in June. The work done on 9 July, a rewritten home page title and a new site clearing page, has not produced clicks yet, and the new page has still not been indexed by Google three weeks after publication. We say more about that below, including what we would do differently.

The other significant piece of July was not visible in any number: the connection carrying your website enquiries had quietly stopped working. We found it, rebuilt it, and recovered the enquiries it had swallowed. That turned out to matter more than we first thought, and there is a section on it below.

Concrete cutting is working. Concrete scanning is not, yet

The two halves of the new campaign are behaving very differently. Concrete and saw cutting took $279.44, ran a 5.11% click rate, and every one of the tracked website enquiries from that campaign landed on the concrete cutting page. GPR and concrete scanning took $200.48, collected the most impressions of any ad group in the account at 532, and converted them at a 2.82% click rate with nothing to show for it.

The search terms explain it. Scanning attracts broad, research-shaped searches: gpr alone was shown 159 times for one click, ground penetrating radar 45 times for one. Cutting attracts people with a job: concrete cutting near me was shown 22 times and clicked 6. In August we will rewrite the scanning ads and page around the job rather than the technology, and if that does not move it we will move the money to cutting.

What the ten paid enquiries actually were

Every one was listened to. Six were real, workable jobs: a soft-cutting enquiry on a large foundation, a directional core through a curb quoted at $750–850, an 85-foot driveway cut quoted at $700, a 40ft x 6ft concrete wall demolition, mudslide debris removal in Yorba Linda with the insurer paying, and an expansion-joint cut that reached a job walk.

Two were not: an automated call, and a Newport Beach concrete haul you judged too small and referred out. The remaining two reached voicemail and left no message, so they cannot be judged either way, but the numbers are still callable.

On six workable jobs, the month cost $180.76 per real job. That is the honest figure to hold the advertising to, and it is the one we will report against every month from here.

Half the paid calls did not reach you first time

Of the ten, three reached you live on that call. Three went to voicemail, one was a no-connect where the line opened and closed, and one was a 22 second call with nothing recorded. Two of the voicemail callers left a full message and both are workable, and one of the no-connect callers rang back an hour later and got to a job walk.

That persistence is lucky, not reliable. At $13.56 a click, a missed call is a paid click thrown away, and this is the cheapest available improvement in the whole account: it needs no more budget and no new pages.

One thing we want to watch: the last week produced nothing

Nine of July's ten paid enquiries arrived by 19 July, and the tenth on 23 July. From 24 to 31 July the account spent $294.54 and produced no paid enquiries at all. This is not a tracking gap: website enquiries from other sources were captured normally on the 25th, 28th, 30th and 31st, so the system was working.

At this volume eight quiet days can be ordinary chance, and we are not going to over-read it. But it is the single thing we most want to see resolved in August, and it is the first thing we will check.

The central problem: the home page is the whole website

All 35 of July's organic clicks went to the home page. Not most of them. All of them. Twelve other pages were shown in Google a combined 129 times between them and earned zero clicks.

That is the single biggest constraint on organic growth here. A site where one page does all the work can only rank for what one page can plausibly be about, and it caps how much of Orange County you can appear in. Everything in the organic plan from here is aimed at getting a second, third and fourth page earning.

The finding that matters most: your flagship page is invisible

The pool demolition page, /pool-demolition/, was not shown in Google's organic results a single time in July. Not shown and not clicked: zero impressions. We checked May, June and the first days of August as well, and the answer is the same in every month. Pool demolition is the service the business is built around, it is the page that converts when people do reach it, and organically it does not exist.

Meanwhile the searches are there. Pool removal, swimming pool removal and pool demolition contractor all appeared in the paid search terms in July and cost around $19 a click. Every one of those clicks could have been free.

This changes the shape of the organic plan. Instead of building more new pages, August starts by working out why the pool page is not being served, and fixing that. It is a bigger prize than anything else on the organic list.

What we tried in July, and why we are calling it a miss

On 9 July we did two things. We rewrote the home page title to target the head terms the site already ranked well for but earned nothing from, and we built a new page, Demolition & Site Clearing Orange County, aimed at a cluster of searches taking around 200 impressions a month at positions 6 to 9 with no page of their own.

Neither has worked so far, and we would rather say that than dress it up. The head terms still convert almost nothing: demolition contractors sat at position 1.0 across 29 impressions in July and produced 3 clicks. The new page is a harder problem: Google has not indexed it at all. It has been live since 9 July, it is in the sitemap, it is linked from the home page, the contact page and the pool page, it returns cleanly and it carries no blocking tag. It has simply never been served in a search result.

The lesson we are taking is that the original theory was wrong. We assumed these were click-rate problems, fixable with better titles. The impression counts say otherwise: strong positions on genuinely thin search volume. Rewriting titles cannot create demand that is not there. August's work moves to where demand demonstrably exists.

The enquiry pipeline was broken

On 31 July we found that the connection carrying website form submissions into your lead tracking had stopped working entirely, and that even when it had been working it was only ever passing across the email address and phone number. The name and the message the customer typed were being dropped. That is why the contact name and message columns in your lead sheet have been empty for months.

Two enquiries were stranded and never arrived at all. Both were recovered and sent to you directly on 31 July: a Santa Ana homeowner with a sinkhole over a filled-in pool, and an enquiry about removing a concrete pool shell.

The connection has been rebuilt from scratch and no longer depends on the third-party service that failed. It now carries the full name, the full message and the service the customer selected, and it runs every fifteen minutes. We also added click-level tracking to every form, so from August we can tell you which advertising click produced which form enquiry.

What we could not measure this month

Your Maps and local listing performance, the calls, direction requests and website clicks it generates, sit in a Google account we do not currently have access to. We would rather leave the section out than estimate it. Getting us view access to the Business Profile is a five minute job and it would add the missing third of the local picture from next month.

        About these numbers

        Every figure in this report was pulled live from source on 5 August 2026. Advertising figures come from the Google Ads API for the calendar month. Enquiry counts come from WhatConverts and from the Google Ads call asset, combined so that neither source is counted twice: website forms and calls to the tracking number come from WhatConverts and always carry a Google click identifier, while calls placed straight from an ad never touch the site and are counted once from Google Ads. Repeat contacts from the same person are counted once. Search clicks, impressions and positions come from Google Search Console for the property southcoastdemo.com. Website visits come from Google Analytics.

        • • Google Ads reports 8 conversions for July against our 10 enquiries. Its figure counts one call twice through two overlapping conversion actions, and misses the enquiries that reached your tracking without registering as a conversion. Ours is reconciled enquiry by enquiry.
        • • Google withholds the rarer searches people used, so the named searches are a sample. Monthly totals are complete.
        • • Search Console counts clicks from Google only. Google Analytics counts sessions from every search engine and counts a returning visitor again. The two are different measures and are not expected to match.
        • • Lead quality is not a model or a score. Every call was listened to and every form read, one at a time, and marked in your lead tracking so you can see the judgement on each one. Where an enquiry could not honestly be judged it is left open rather than counted either way.
        • • One internal test submission made on 31 July while verifying the new tracking is excluded from every figure in this report and from your lead sheet.
        • • Google Business Profile performance is absent because we do not have access to the listing. No figure in this report estimates it.
        • • Where a change was made at the end of July, it is dated. Work done on 31 July does not explain July's results.
