The month in short

July was a much smaller month than June, by design. The advertising was deliberately scaled back in the first week. Spend fell from $7,191.88 to $1,975.20, a drop of 72.5%. Leads fell with it, from 103 to 54.

The cost per lead improved, and we do not want you to read that as a win. It fell from $69.82 to $36.58, but that is what happens when you switch off the two most expensive campaigns and leave the cheapest one running. It is a consequence of spending less, not of the advertising working harder. The honest summary of July is that the account did less of everything.

The reason is straightforward: this was the month MCI Waste was introduced, and the paid budget moved with it. The main Search campaign was reduced to a $1.00 per day budget on 3 July, and Local Services Ads ran for the first five days and then stopped serving for the remaining 26. Nothing was misconfigured and nothing here needs correcting. It does mean July's figures describe a deliberately smaller account, and should not be read as a fall in performance.

The one campaign that ran normally all month was the newer AI Max Search campaign. It carried the month on its own: $1,397.04 of the $1,975.20 spent, and 44 of the 54 leads, at $31.75 each.

What happened to the two quiet campaigns. The main Search campaign spent $156.26 in the first two days of July and $17.18 across the remaining 29. Local Services Ads served impressions on 1–5 July and then nothing at all from 6 July to the end of the month. Both are the result of the deliberate pull-back described above rather than any fault, and both are verifiable day by day in the account.

Local Services Ads is worth a closer look once it is running again. At $80.94 per lead it is the most expensive line in the account, but Local Services leads are people who called a verified, Google-badged listing directly, and it produced 35 leads in June when it ran for most of the month. It is a strong channel that was switched off for most of July by choice, not because of anything wrong with it. Worth revisiting next month, once the ongoing Mr. Cheapee budget is settled.

The AI Max campaign is now the account. It is a newer campaign type that lets Google match a wider range of searches than a traditional keyword list, and it has been the cheapest source of leads for two months running. Its main constraint in July was budget: it lost roughly a third of the available searches simply because the daily budget ran out.

Lead quality

Every enquiry from July was listened to or read and marked as a real job or not. This is the first month we have done that for the full month.

The 20 we ruled out were not lost business. They were five “your Google listing has been flagged” scam robocalls, two people looking for work, wrong numbers, an insurance broker, a marketing company selling review services through your contact form twice, and two test submissions of our own. The 51 we could not assess were calls that ended before anyone spoke or voicemails where the caller left no message, so there was nothing to judge.

Of the calls we could assess, 83% were genuine jobs. That is a healthy ratio and it says the advertising is bringing in the right kind of enquiry, not noise.

A note on the Facebook enquiries. Fourteen of July's enquiries arrived tagged as coming from your Facebook advertising campaign, which Shapeshift does not manage. We have excluded them from the Google figures above so they cannot flatter the numbers.

The profile was shown slightly more often than in June but produced slightly fewer actions — 237 against 250. The share of people who did something after seeing you fell from 34.7% to 31.9%.

Maps visibility is driven mostly by proximity, review count and review score, which leads directly to the next section.

You appear in the map pack as often as your closest competitor and you have more reviews than either of them. The gap is the score, not the count: 4.2 against Bin There Dump That's 4.9.

That is an unusually clear finding, because it tells us what not to spend effort on. The usual advice is to chase more reviews. You already have the most. The lever here is the rating. Moving from 4.2 to 4.5 would do more for your Maps visibility than another twenty reviews at the current average, and it is also the number a customer compares before they call.

Organically the picture is better still. Across the same ten searches the site ranks for nine, with a best position of second. The one search it does not appear for at all is “dumpster rental Yonkers”, where a competitor whose entire domain name is built around that phrase holds seven of the ten. That is a specific, fixable gap and there is a page planned for it.

About these numbers

Every figure in this report was pulled live from source on 6 August 2026. Advertising figures come from the Google Ads API for the calendar month. Google Business Profile figures come from Google's own Business Profile data. The local map pack comparison was scanned on 6 August across ten Westchester dumpster searches.
